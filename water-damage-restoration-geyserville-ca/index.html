For Geyserville, the first step is a call to (707) 221-5171. We work from Windsor, about twelve road miles south, and dispatch stays on duty around the clock.

Geyserville is an unincorporated community in Sonoma County, with 861 residents at the 2020 census and ZIP code 95441. State Route 128 runs through town as Geyserville Avenue and joins US 101 to the north.

How a twelve-mile call works

Geyserville is the farthest town on our list, and the drive up US 101 is longer than a call in Windsor. That matters for the way we handle the call. Dispatch asks a few more questions up front so that the crew arrives with the right equipment, and we tell you honestly what we can do and when.

Stop the source first. Then tell dispatch what is wet and how much. If it is a small leak, your answers help us decide what to bring, such as extraction units, air movers or a dehumidifier.

When water is spreading in Geyserville

If water is moving across a floor, through a ceiling or out of a drain, call the line at (707) 221-5171 before you do anything complicated. Dispatch will ask where the water started, how much there is and whether anything electrical is wet.

Give the street name and any landmark that helps a driver find you, since rural addresses can be hard to see at night. Mention gates, long driveways and animals so the crew knows what to expect.

Geyserville and its setting

Hot springs and steam vents were found in 1847 on the Rancho Tzabaco land grant, and the town was first called Clairville before it became Geyserville. It sits at about 213 feet of elevation near the Russian River valley.

Water here can come from the roof in a winter storm, a supply line in an older building or a water heater in a utility closet. The storm water damage page covers rain that comes through the building, and the water heater leak page covers tanks.

Working with rural properties

Many properties around Geyserville are larger lots, vineyards or farm buildings. We do not have data on how many use wells or septic systems, so ask your plumber about yours. If a septic or sewer line is backing up, read the sewage backup page before you touch anything.

For outbuildings, barns and tasting rooms, the commercial water damage page explains how we scope a larger structure.

If water is still spreading: steps before the crew gets there

Close the line

Shut the nearest valve, or the main where the line enters the house. On a well system, switch off the pump only if you can reach the switch safely.

Stay out of puddles near power

Keep your hands off outlets, panels and cords near water, and report any electrical hazard to the utility and 911.

Lift and clear

Raise rugs and small items. Clear a path for equipment.

Keep contaminated water away

Septic, drain and outside water are not clean. Keep people and animals out of it.

Photograph the damage

Capture wide and close shots, and note the time you found the water.

Call dispatch

Dial (707) 221-5171 any hour of the day or night.

Permits and insurance

Geyserville has no city hall, so repairs that need a permit go through the county, not a town office. Ask Permit Sonoma before ordering materials. For coverage, a policy usually treats a sudden failure and a slow seep differently, so call your insurer first.
