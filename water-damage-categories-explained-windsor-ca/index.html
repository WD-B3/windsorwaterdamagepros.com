Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained for Windsor, CA Homeowners

What clean, gray and black water mean, how water changes category over time and what that means for your belongings.

By the Windsor Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

When a restoration crew says the water is Category 2, they mean something specific. The categories describe how contaminated the water is and how carefully it has to be handled. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for these definitions.

This guide explains the three categories in plain terms and shows how they affect what is kept, cleaned or removed.

The three categories at a glance

CategoryCommon nameTypical sourcesTypical handling
1Clean waterBroken supply line, tub overflow with clean water, rainwater from a clean sourceExtract and dry, most materials can be saved
2Gray waterDishwasher or washing machine discharge, toilet overflow with urine only, sump pump waterExtract, clean and dry, porous materials often removed
3Black waterSewage, river or creek flooding, water from outside carrying soilRemove porous materials, clean and sanitize

These are general descriptions. A professional decides the category on site based on the source and the conditions.

Category 1: clean water at the source

Category 1 water comes from a sanitary source, such as a supply pipe. It is not harmful to people at the moment it escapes. It becomes a concern as it sits.

Most materials can be dried in place if the job starts quickly. Carpet may be saved, pad may be replaced and drywall can often be dried if it has not softened. The burst pipe page shows how a Category 1 job usually unfolds.

Category 2: gray water

Category 2 water contains contaminants that can cause illness if people are exposed to them. A dishwasher or washing machine discharge, or a toilet overflow without feces, are typical sources.

Porous materials such as carpet pad and insulation are often removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned and dried. Because gray water can worsen with time, quick action matters.

Category 3: black water

Category 3 water is grossly contaminated and can carry harmful organisms. Sewage, river flooding and ground-level water from outside are typical sources. Water that has entered from outside and picked up soil is also treated as Category 3.

Porous materials that touched it are generally removed, and structural materials are cleaned and sanitized before drying. Protective equipment is part of the job. See the sewage backup page and the flood damage page.

How water changes category

Water that starts as Category 1 can move to Category 2 or 3 if it sits for a long time, passes through dirty materials or mixes with other substances. Warm conditions speed that change.

That is why the clock matters even for a clean pipe leak. The sooner the water comes out, the more of the building you can keep.

What the category means for your belongings

  • Category 1: furniture, books and clothing can often be dried or washed
  • Category 2: washable items can often be cleaned, porous items such as upholstered furniture may be at risk
  • Category 3: porous items that were soaked are usually discarded, hard items can be cleaned and sanitized

Photograph what you discard and make a list. That record helps if you file an insurance claim.

Insurance and categories

Insurers pay attention to the category because it affects the scope and cost of work. Category 3 water from outside is usually a flood, which is generally excluded from standard homeowners policies. Check your policy.

A quick way to think about the risk

If you can answer three questions, you can usually guess the category. Where did the water start, what did it cross on the way and how long has it been there? Clean source, short trip and short time points to Category 1. A dirty source or a long wait moves the answer upward.

Why the label is a plan, not a verdict

Hearing that water is Category 3 sounds alarming, but it simply tells the crew which protective steps and disposal rules apply. It also tells you which belongings are at risk. A clear label early in the job lets everyone plan the work and the paperwork.

Creek and river water near Windsor

Mark West Creek reached moderate flood stage in mid-January 2023, according to Sonoma County storm updates. Water from a creek that enters a home carries soil and contaminants, so it is treated as Category 3.

For the Russian River, NOAA's California Nevada River Forecast Center lists a flood stage of 32 feet at Guerneville. Windsor sits about two miles from the river, and the FEMA map plus a call to town staff will tell you about your own parcel.

Questions readers ask

Can clean water become dangerous?

Yes. Over time, clean water can pick up contaminants from building materials and grow bacteria. Quick drying keeps it from getting worse.

Is rainwater Category 1?

Not always. Rainwater that falls on a roof and enters through the building can pick up dirt. Water that flows across the ground is treated as Category 3.

How do restoration crews decide the category?

They look at the source, how long the water sat, what it touched and the conditions on site. They follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Is water from a washing machine Category 2?

Often yes, because the discharge carries detergent and soil. A supply hose failure delivers clean water.

What should Windsor homeowners do after a creek overflow?

Treat the water as Category 3, stay out of it, call a restoration company and check your policy for flood coverage.
