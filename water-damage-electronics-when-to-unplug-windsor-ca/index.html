What Water Damage Does to Electronics and When to Unplug: A Windsor, CA Guide

Safety first, then salvage. How to handle wet outlets, appliances, computers and phones.

By the Windsor Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

Water and electricity create a real risk of shock and fire. The first rule is that nobody should stand in water to reach a switch or unplug a cord. The second rule is that wet devices should not be powered on to test them.

This guide covers the safe order of operations. When in doubt, call your electric utility and 911 for hazards.

Safety first

Electrical hazards

Do not touch outlets, cords or appliances if you are standing in water or if water is near them. Do not use the main breaker if you must step in water to reach it. If you see sparks, smell burning or hear buzzing, leave the area and call 911 and the utility.

If the panel is dry and you can reach it safely, turn off the breakers for wet rooms. If it is not safe, call an electrician or the utility.

Unplug only from a dry spot

If a device is wet but you are on a dry surface and the outlet is dry, unplug it. Do not unplug something while standing in water, and do not touch a wet power strip.

Remove batteries from wet small devices if you can do so safely. Do not charge or turn on a wet device.

What water does to devices

DeviceRiskFirst step
Phone or tabletCorrosion insidePower off, remove case, do not charge
LaptopShort circuits and corrosionPower off, unplug, remove battery if possible
TV or monitorShorts in the boardUnplug and do not power on
Small applianceMotor and board damageUnplug and have it checked
Large applianceControl boards and wiringCut power at the breaker if safe
Power strips and cordsShock riskDiscard if soaked

Corrosion can show up days after a device dries. A device that works today may fail next week.

Drying small devices

Wipe the outside, open any compartments and let the device air dry in a ventilated space. Silica gel packets can help, but uncooked rice is not reliable. Avoid heat, which can warp parts and damage batteries.

Allow at least a couple of days before trying to power on a wet device. Better still, ask a repair shop to open it and clean the board.

Outlets, switches and wiring

Outlets and switches that were soaked should be checked by a qualified electrician before they are used. Wet wiring inside walls can fail later. A restoration crew can tell you where the water traveled, and an electrician decides what is safe to use.

If water came through a ceiling fixture, do not turn on the light. The ceiling water damage page explains why.

Appliances and HVAC equipment

Refrigerators, washers and dryers can be damaged by flood water around their bases. An appliance technician can check the controls and motor. Furnaces and water heaters in crawl spaces or basements need professional inspection before restart.

See the water heater page for what to do if the tank leaked near controls.

Insurance and electronics

Electronics are often covered as personal property if the loss is covered. Photograph models and serial numbers, and keep receipts. Do not discard before your insurer has a chance to review, unless safety requires it.

Chargers, batteries and power banks

Lithium batteries are sensitive to water. A power bank or battery pack that was submerged should be taken out of service and disposed of at a battery recycling point. Do not charge it to see if it works.

Wet chargers and cords can leave a film on the contacts. Replacing them is cheap compared with the risk of a short.

Data backup before repair

If a computer or phone holds data you need, tell the repair shop before they begin. Data recovery works best when the device is powered off quickly and not tested. Back up what you can from other devices and cloud accounts.

Keep receipts and model numbers handy for the insurance claim and for questions to the manufacturer.

Document electronics for your claim

List each damaged device with its brand, model, age and price, and photograph the label. Keep any manufacturer paperwork that shows the purchase date. A tidy list lets an adjuster process the contents portion of your claim faster.

A safe order of operations

Work from least to most risky. Get people out of the wet area, deal with the breaker only if it is dry around it, unplug devices from dry outlets, and leave anything that sits in water for an electrician. Rushing to rescue a television is never worth a shock.

A Windsor note: storms, outages and surges

Winter storms can bring outages and surges as well as leaks. If power returns while parts of your home are wet, have an electrician check the circuits before you use them.

Report downed lines to your utility and 911, and keep clear. For electrical repairs that go beyond swapping a device, ask Town of Windsor building staff whether a permit applies.

Questions readers ask

Should I put a wet phone in rice?

Rice is not reliable. Power the phone off, remove the case, wipe it and let it dry in a ventilated space. A repair shop can clean the board.

Can I use an outlet that got wet?

Not until an electrician checks it. Wet outlets can short.

Should I turn off the main breaker?

Only if you can reach it from a dry spot. If not, call the utility or an electrician.

Is a wet power strip safe?

No. Discard it.

Who checks electrical work after a leak in Windsor?

A licensed electrician. Ask Town of Windsor building staff about permits.
