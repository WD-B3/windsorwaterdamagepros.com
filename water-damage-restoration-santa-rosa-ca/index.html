Santa Rosa is the county seat and the largest city in Sonoma County, with 178,127 residents at the 2020 census. The city's ZIP codes in our service list are 95401 through 95406.

Homes built in different eras

A city of this size has every kind of house. Older homes may have galvanized or mixed-metal supply lines and original drain pipes, while newer subdivisions rely on plastic supply lines and appliance hoses. Large parts of Coffey Park and Fountaingrove burned in the October 2017 Tubbs Fire and were rebuilt, so many homes there are newer than their neighbors.

The age of your plumbing tells you where to look. In an older house, start with the burst pipe page. In a newer one, a failed appliance hose is more likely, and the appliance leak page explains what to do.

Downtown, Railroad Square and commercial buildings

Railroad Square, west of US 101, has the highest concentration of historic commercial buildings in the city. Water in an old commercial building can run through floors, walls and shared ceilings, and a tenant may not know where it started.

Owners of older storefronts can read how a tenant loss is protected and written up on the commercial page. For multi-unit buildings, the ceiling repair page shows what to expect when a leak from above comes through.

Santa Rosa Creek and the rain record

Santa Rosa Creek rises on Hood Mountain, runs through the heart of the city and flows toward the Laguna de Santa Rosa. The Prince Memorial Greenway follows the creek. The city averages roughly 31 to 32 inches of rain a year, nearly all of it in winter.

Water from the creek, a storm drain or the street counts as flood, which standard policies tend to leave out. Check the FEMA map for your address before you assume anything about coverage.

When water is spreading in Santa Rosa

In a large city, calls come in from houses, apartments and offices at the same time, especially during storms. Give dispatch your exact address, the floor, and whether the water is clean or from a drain. We send crews as they become available and never promise an arrival time.

Emergency prep: what to do while you wait

Close the valve

Apartment buildings often have a unit shutoff. Ask the building manager if the leak is in a shared line.

Power safety

Turn off power to wet rooms only if you can do so safely. Never reach for a breaker while standing in water.

Move the valuables

Electronics, shoes, books and papers go to a dry, high spot.

Keep people out

Drain and toilet water is not clean. Keep children and pets away from it.

Contact your landlord

If you rent, tell the landlord or property manager and keep a record of the date and time.

Use the 24/7 line

Call dispatch at (707) 221-5171 and describe the room.

Insurance, permits and renters

Renters and owners both carry policies that respond to sudden water, and both usually leave out flood. Read yours and call your insurer. Inside the city, permits are a City of Santa Rosa matter, and in unincorporated areas around it they go through Permit Sonoma.

If you rent, your landlord's insurance typically covers the building and your renters policy covers your belongings. Photograph your possessions and keep receipts for emergency steps.
