If a line under your floor is leaking, the water can lift flooring, wet baseboards and seep into walls. This page explains what a slab leak is, what the plumber does, and what the restoration crew dries afterward.

Warm floor spots, a high water bill or running-water sounds

Homeowners searching for slab leak signs usually describe the same things: a hot patch on the floor, damp carpet with no source, a meter that keeps turning when every fixture is off, a musty smell and the sound of water running in the walls. None of these proves a slab leak, but together they are a reason to call a plumber for leak detection.

A slab leak is a leak in a supply line that runs under or inside the concrete slab. Because the water is under the floor, the first damage is usually to flooring and the base of walls. Restoration work is needed once the plumber stops the water, and it is the focus of this page.

What to do if you suspect a slab leak

  1. Turn off every fixture and check the water meter. If the dial keeps moving, water is flowing somewhere.
  2. If water is surfacing or the floor is hot and wet, close the main shutoff and call a plumber who offers leak detection.
  3. Lift rugs and furniture off damp areas and photograph the floor.
  4. Call dispatch if floors, baseboards or walls are wet so crews can map moisture and start drying.

Hot water and electricity

A leak in a hot water line can make a floor hot enough to burn skin, and water that surfaces near outlets can create a shock risk. Keep people and pets off the area, and call 911 for any electrical concern.

How common are slab foundations in Windsor?

We do not have a Windsor-specific figure for how many homes sit on slabs versus raised foundations, and we do not claim slab leaks are common here. The Sonoma County assessor or a home inspection report will tell you what your house is built on. If you do have a slab, supply lines run under or through it and can fail from corrosion, pressure or movement.

That is why this page is written around how a slab leak works rather than around a local frequency. Your inspection report is the right source for what your own foundation is.

How a slab leak gets found and fixed

A plumber uses acoustic listening devices, pressure tests or thermal imaging to find the leak, then repairs it by spot excavation through the slab, re-routing the line overhead or replacing the line. The method depends on the layout and the condition of the pipes, and it is the plumber's decision.

The restoration crew arrives before or after the repair depending on how bad the water is. When water is surfacing, extraction and drying start as soon as the water is stopped. When the damage is hidden, readings guide how far moisture traveled under flooring and into walls.

Concrete holds moisture for a long time, so slab drying can take longer than drying wood framing. Moisture readings on the slab itself and on the wall bases tell the crew when to stop. The process is explained on the structural drying page.

Questions about slab leaks

Do you respond to suspected slab leaks outside office hours?

Dispatch takes the call at any hour and helps you decide whether to shut the main. Drying is arranged once the leak is located and stopped by a plumber.

Do you repair the slab leak itself?

No. A plumber finds and repairs the line. We remove water and dry the flooring and walls that got wet.

Is a slab leak covered by insurance?

Coverage varies widely. Some policies cover the resulting water damage but not the cost of reaching the pipe. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

How do I know if my home has a slab foundation?

A home inspection report, the Sonoma County assessor record or the builder's plans will say. A crawl space or basement means you do not have a slab under that part of the house.

Can a slab leak cause mold?

Persistent moisture under flooring can support mold growth, so fast repair and drying matter. This site does not offer mold testing or remediation.
