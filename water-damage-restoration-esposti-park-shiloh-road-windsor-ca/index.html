Water Damage Restoration in Esposti Park and Shiloh Road, Windsor, CA

Water in a home or apartment near Esposti Park and Shiloh Road? Call dispatch and say what is wet and where it started.

Residents near Esposti Park and Shiloh Road in Windsor can call (707) 221-5171 when water gets into a home, apartment or shop. We are based in Windsor, and this corner of town, where Old Redwood Highway meets Shiloh Road, is part of the area we serve.

The Town of Windsor filed a notice in August 2026 for a playground project at Esposti Park, which it places at 6000 Old Redwood Highway at Shiloh Road on about 10 acres. The park is the clearest landmark for the surrounding blocks.

Homes, apartments and shops around the park

A 2014 filing with the Town describes a request for a 36-unit apartment complex on Old Redwood Highway, in the area covered by the Shiloh Road Village Vision Plan. The Town's plans point to denser housing along this stretch, and the area also includes single-family streets and shopping along Shiloh Road.

That mix means three kinds of calls: a house with a failed appliance line, an apartment where a leak crossed a floor, and a shop with water on a hard floor. Each needs different handling, and the first call should tell us which one it is.

What the Town records about water here

The Town's hazard mitigation plan lists several localized flooding spots in Windsor, among them Dawn Way near Old Redwood Highway, Shiloh Road and Caletti Avenue, and Shiloh Road at the US 101 southbound on-ramp. These are places where water has pooled on roads, and the plan does not say homes flood there.

Pool Creek is also named in the plan, at Windsor Road, and in the 2014 apartment filing as a waterway for the Old Redwood Highway site. A creek on a record does not mean a particular street floods. If you want the answer for your property, the FEMA map and the Town are the right places to ask.

How we respond to a typical call

  1. You call and describe the source and the wet rooms.
  2. Dispatch gives the first safe steps and arranges a crew.
  3. The crew extracts standing water and checks walls, floors and ceilings with a moisture meter.
  4. Drying equipment is set and the written scope and price are explained before work starts.
  5. Readings are repeated until the structure is dry.

No arrival time is promised on the phone. What we promise is that dispatch will tell you what to do while you wait.

Services most relevant near Shiloh Road

A leak that came through a roof or wall in wet weather is a storm water job. A pipe failure in an apartment starts with burst pipe cleanup, and a leak in a ground-floor shop may fall under commercial water damage.

For standing water on a floor, water extraction is the first step. After that, the structural drying page explains the long part of the job.

Insurance and flood maps

A planned mixed-use project of 225 homes on Shiloh Road is noted in the hazard plan as lying partly in a special flood hazard area. That is about one project, and it says nothing about the homes around it. Look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and treat what you find as the mapped answer for that address only.

Ask your insurer about water coverage before you need it. Flood and sudden pipe damage are handled differently, and your policy controls which applies.

Questions from Esposti Park and Shiloh Road residents

Is emergency water removal available near Esposti Park?

Yes. The line at (707) 221-5171 is answered at all hours. Describe what is wet and whether it has stopped, and dispatch will arrange a crew without quoting an arrival time.

Is the Shiloh Road area prone to flooding?

The Town lists a few road intersections there as localized flooding spots. It does not say that homes flood, and we have not verified the flood zone for any home.

Do you handle apartments as well as houses?

Yes, with access to the unit. Tell dispatch about the unit below if water dripped through, and contact the property manager early.

Water came in during a storm. Is that covered?

Only your policy can answer that. Rainwater that enters a roof or wall and flood water rising from outside are often treated differently, so call your insurer and keep your photos.

What should I photograph?

The source, every wet room, damaged belongings and any moisture readings. Take the pictures before moving things if it is safe.
