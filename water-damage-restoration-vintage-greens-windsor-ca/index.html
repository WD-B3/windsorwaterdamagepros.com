Water Damage Restoration in Vintage Greens, Windsor, CA

A pipe, a water heater or a washer let go in a Vintage Greens home. Call dispatch, say what is wet and whether the water has stopped.

  • 95492
Call (707) 221-5171

Vintage Greens homeowners who find water on a floor or a stain spreading across a ceiling can call (707) 221-5171 and talk to dispatch right away. We are a Windsor company, and Vintage Greens is a Windsor subdivision we know by its streets.

The Town of Windsor reviewed the Vintage Greens Subdivision in 2000. The state record describes about 445 homes and a 10-acre community soccer park on roughly 119 acres at Windsor Road and Wilson Lane. Local real estate guides place it next to the Windsor Golf Club and describe it as built in the early 2000s.

A planned subdivision of single-family homes

Because Vintage Greens was planned as one subdivision, its houses tend to share a layout family and a similar mix of kitchens, laundry rooms and water heater closets. That does not tell us any home has a problem. It does mean a crew that has seen one floor plan will recognize the others.

We do not assume anything about plumbing materials or past damage in a particular street. The first visit finds out what is wet, what the source was and how far the water traveled.

Where water comes from in a house like this

  • A washing machine supply hose or drain that has come loose
  • A dishwasher or refrigerator line under a kitchen counter
  • A water heater that leaks from the tank or its fittings
  • An upstairs bathroom supply line or toilet seal
  • A slow drip inside a wall that shows first as a stain

Each of those has its own page. The water heater leak page and the appliance leak page explain what we do first, and the kitchen page covers cabinets and flooring.

What the town record does and does not say

The Town's 2000 negative declaration listed flood plain, drainage and hydrology among the issues it considered for the project. That is a checklist of topics reviewed during approval, and it does not say Vintage Greens floods. We mention it so you know that drainage was part of the planning, and we make no claim about any street.

We have not confirmed a FEMA flood zone for Vintage Greens. A mapped zone applies to a property and not to a whole subdivision, so check your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center before drawing conclusions either way.

Dry the house, not just the surface

Water in a finished home soaks into drywall, insulation, baseboards and the layer under flooring. Wiping the top and running a household fan leaves moisture inside. Crews place air movers and a dehumidifier, take readings and come back until the numbers drop.

The carpet page and the drywall page explain what can be saved and what gets cut out. Some materials cannot be dried in place, and we say so on the visit.

Keep a record for your insurer

Take photos of the source and every wet room before moving things. Write down when you noticed the water and when you shut it off. Ask your insurer what it needs and keep every invoice, because coverage depends on the cause and on your policy.

We provide a written scope and moisture readings that you can hand to your adjuster. We do not decide coverage and we cannot promise a claim result.

Neighborhood notes for Vintage Greens homeowners

Golf course frontage and greenbelts are part of the setting that local guides describe. If water is coming in from outside after heavy rain, call the same number and describe where it enters, because the cleanup is different from an indoor pipe leak.

If an HOA manages shared landscaping or drainage in your section, tell them as well. A restorer cleans and dries your home. Fixing an outside drain is a job for the party that owns it.

Questions from Vintage Greens homeowners

Is emergency water removal available in Vintage Greens?

Yes, the line takes calls around the clock. Say which room is wet, what the source was and whether it is off, and dispatch will walk you through what to do until a crew can be assigned.

Is Vintage Greens in a FEMA flood zone?

We did not verify that for the neighborhood. Check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and remember a mapped zone applies to the property and not to the whole subdivision.

My water heater is leaking in the garage. What first?

Turn off the water to the heater if you can reach the valve safely, and switch off power or gas to it only if it is safe. Then call and describe how far the water has spread.

Can the carpet be saved after a clean-water leak?

Sometimes. It depends on how long it was wet and how deep the water went. Contaminated water usually means removal, and the crew will explain which case you have.

Do you work with insurance paperwork?

We supply photos, moisture readings and a written scope of work. Whether the claim is paid is the insurer's decision under your policy.
