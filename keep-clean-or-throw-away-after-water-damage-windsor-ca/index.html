What to Keep, Clean or Throw Away After Water Damage: A Windsor, CA Guide

A sorting framework by water type and material, so you do not toss what can be saved or keep what is unsafe.

By the Windsor Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

After water gets into a home, the question is what to do with everything it touched. Two things decide the answer: how clean the water was and how porous the item is. Hard, nonporous things survive more often than soft, absorbent ones.

Photograph before you discard, and keep a list. Your insurer may ask for it.

Start with the water type

Clean water from a supply line is the most forgiving. Gray water from appliances is riskier, and sewage or flood water makes most soft items unsafe. If you are unsure, treat the water as the worse type and ask for advice.

Items that should go

  • Food, drinks and medicine that touched floodwater or sewage
  • Pillows, mattresses and stuffed toys that were soaked
  • Particleboard furniture that has swelled
  • Cosmetics and baby items that touched contaminated water
  • Anything that smells after cleaning

Throwing out a good item hurts, but keeping an unsafe one can hurt more. When in doubt, photograph, list and discard.

Items you can often save

Solid wood furniture, metal tools, glass, ceramics and many plastics clean up well. Wash with soap and water, then dry. For contaminated water, add a disinfectant suitable for the material and follow its label.

Washable fabrics can often be laundered. Items that are dry-clean only, or valuable, may be worth professional cleaning.

Contents cleaning and pack-out

Some restoration companies move belongings out of the house for cleaning and storage. Ask whether the company offers it, who does the work and how items are tracked. Keep a list of what leaves the house.

Document before you discard

  1. Photograph each item, including labels.
  2. Write a list with age, brand and cost.
  3. Keep a sample of carpet or fabric if the adjuster wants proof.
  4. Ask your insurer before discarding large items.
  5. Keep receipts for replacement.

Health and safety

Wear gloves and sturdy shoes. Keep children, elderly people and pets away from contaminated items. Wash hands and clothing after handling. If someone feels ill after cleanup, call a doctor.

How to clean items that can be saved

  1. Move the item to a dry, ventilated area away from the wet room.
  2. Wipe or rinse off mud and debris with clean water.
  3. Wash with warm water and mild soap.
  4. Rinse and let it dry completely before storing.
  5. Disinfect only if the water was contaminated and the label allows it.

Do not mix cleaning products, especially bleach with ammonia. Work in a ventilated space and wear gloves.

Special cases: kids, pets and kitchens

Toys, pacifiers and bottles that touched contaminated water are better replaced. Pet bedding can be washed hot if the water was clean. In the kitchen, pots and metal utensils can be washed and sanitized, while porous wood spoons and cutting boards that soaked in dirty water are usually thrown out.

Canned food in undamaged cans can sometimes be saved after washing and sanitizing the cans, but ask a food safety source for the current rules. When in doubt, discard.

Estimating value for your insurer

Your insurer may value items at replacement cost or at actual cash value, depending on your policy. Write down the age and condition of each item. Receipts, online order histories and photos of the room before the loss all help.

Keep a running total as you go. A simple spreadsheet with item, room, age, price and a note on whether it was saved or discarded makes the conversation with your adjuster easier.

Keeping the work manageable

Sorting a whole house is tiring. Work one room at a time, set up three clear areas for keep, clean and discard, and take breaks. Ask a friend to handle the photographing and listing while you do the lifting. A calm pace prevents mistakes with items that matter.

A Windsor note: hauling and recovery information

After large storms, damaged contents can pile up faster than usual. Ask your waste hauler about rules for bulky items, and check socoemergency.org for county recovery notices.

If your loss is part of a declared event, ask your insurer whether additional living expenses apply, and keep receipts for storage and cleaning.

Questions readers ask

Can I wash clothes that got wet from a leak?

If the water was clean, yes, as soon as possible. If it was contaminated, wash hot or discard.

Should I throw out a wet mattress?

Usually, if it is soaked. Mattresses dry very slowly and can hold contamination.

How do I save wet books or photos?

Dry them carefully, or freeze them to stop damage until you can handle them. Ask a professional for valuable items.

Is pack-out covered by insurance?

It can be, depending on your policy. Ask your insurer.

Where do Windsor residents find debris information?

Ask your waste hauler and check the Sonoma County emergency site for recovery notices.
