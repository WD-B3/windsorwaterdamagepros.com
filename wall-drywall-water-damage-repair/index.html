Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Windsor, CA

Stains, bubbling paint, swollen baseboards or a soft wall in your Windsor home. We dry what can be saved and cut out what cannot.

Call (707) 221-5171

Drywall is paper-faced gypsum, and water hurts it fast. A wall that wicked water from a wet floor, a leaking pipe inside the cavity or a roof leak running down a stud can show bubbling paint, soft baseboards, brown stains or crumbling edges. A wet wall is worth a quick call to (707) 221-5171, because paint hides what drywall and framing are holding.

This page covers drywall, baseboards and the wall cavity. Floors and ceilings are on other pages, and the source of the water, whether a pipe, an appliance or a roof, is a separate repair.

Signs of water damage in a wall

  • Bubbling, peeling or cracked paint
  • Brown or yellow stains, often with a ring at the edge
  • Soft, swollen or darkened baseboards
  • Drywall that feels spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • A musty smell or a wall that feels cool and damp

A water stain is not always a current leak. Old stains can be dry, and new stains can start small. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera tell you whether the wall is wet now.

What to do when a wall gets wet

  1. Find and stop the source if you can, such as a supply valve or a leaking fixture.
  2. Move furniture and belongings away from the wall.
  3. Do not paint over a wet or stained wall.
  4. Photograph the wall and mark the edge of the stain with pencil and a date so you can see if it grows.
  5. Call dispatch for readings and drying.

Can wet drywall be dried or must it be cut out?

ConditionUsual path
Damp but firm, clean water, caught quicklyDry in place with air movers and dehumidifiers
Soft, swollen or crumblingCut out and replace
Contaminated water, such as sewage or flood waterCut out above the waterline
Water in the cavity behind firm drywallOpen small holes, dry the cavity, then repair

A flood cut removes drywall a set height above the waterline, usually a foot or two, so that wicked moisture is not left inside. Insulation behind the cut is removed as well.

How the wall is dried and repaired

Crews map moisture across the wall, remove baseboards and drill small holes so air can reach the cavity. Air movers blow into the cavity while dehumidifiers pull water out of the air. Wet insulation is removed because it holds water against the framing.

After drying, repair follows in order: new drywall, taping and texturing, primer and paint. Priming matters because it blocks stains from bleeding back through. For how drying is monitored, see structural drying.

Baseboards, trim and paint

Baseboards are often the first thing to swell and are often the first to be removed so the wall can dry. MDF trim swells and does not return, while solid wood trim can be dried and reused. Paint that bubbled must be removed before repainting, and a matching sheen helps a patch disappear. A written scope should say who paints and to what extent.

Where the water usually comes from

Walls get wet from leaking supply lines inside the cavity, wicking from a wet floor, a roof or window leak running down a stud and appliance or shower leaks. Tracing the source is part of the job. For supply failures, see burst pipe damage, and for roof or upstairs leaks see ceiling water damage repair.

Questions about wall and drywall water damage

Do I need to wait for daylight to call about a wet wall?

No. Shut off the likely source, mark the stain edge with pencil and call. Dispatch answers at any hour.

Can I paint over a water stain?

Only when the wall is fully dry and the source is fixed. Paint over a wet stain tends to bubble and bleed again.

How do I know if the wall is still wet?

A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera tell you. Stains and smells alone do not.

Is wet drywall covered by insurance?

A burst pipe behind drywall is often covered, while a long seep may not be. Ask your insurer which one you had.

Who fixes the pipe?

A plumber. We dry and plan repairs to the wall.
