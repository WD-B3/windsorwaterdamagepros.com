What to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company Before You Hire (Windsor, CA)

A list of questions that separates a clear plan from a vague promise, written for homeowners with water in the house.

By the Windsor Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

When water is spreading, you may have five minutes to choose someone. These questions are short enough to ask on the phone and specific enough to tell you a lot. They apply to any company, including ours.

A good company will answer plainly. A hesitant answer is information too.

Licensing and insurance

California requires a contractor's license for work of $1,000 or more in labor and materials, according to the Contractors State License Board. Ask whether the company holds a license for the work it will do, and look the license up on the CSLB website yourself.

Ask whether the company carries liability insurance and workers' compensation insurance, and ask for proof. Do not rely on a verbal yes.

Scope and process

  • What will you do on the first visit?
  • How will you decide what to remove and what to dry?
  • What equipment will you use, and how many days will it run?
  • How will you measure that the structure is dry?
  • Who will be on site, and who supervises?

The answer should mention moisture meters, air movers and dehumidifiers, along with a plan to monitor readings. A company that cannot describe how it knows the job is done is guessing. The structural drying page describes this in detail.

Pricing and paperwork

Ask how the price is set. Some jobs are priced by the hour, some by line items and some by equipment days. Ask for a written scope and price before work starts, and ask what could change the number.

Be careful with any request to sign a blank form or a broad authorization. Read every page. An assignment of benefits or a direction to pay can affect your claim, so ask what each document does.

Insurance help

Ask what documentation the company provides for your claim. Moisture logs, photos and a written scope are useful. Ask whether they bill your insurer directly or you pay and request reimbursement.

Beware of a promise that the insurer will cover everything. Only the insurer decides coverage, and the company cannot guarantee it.

Response and availability

Ask whether someone answers the phone around the clock and who you will reach. Be wary of a firm arrival time that sounds too good. A trustworthy company explains what it will do, tells you when it can arrive honestly and updates you if that changes.

Red flags

  • Pressure to sign immediately
  • A price that changes without a written reason
  • No license number or a number that does not check out
  • A request for full payment before work begins
  • Door-to-door sales after a storm
  • Refusal to put anything in writing

What to do with the answers

Write the answers down, compare two or three companies if time allows and choose the one that answers plainly and documents its work. In an emergency, begin with a company that answers the phone and gives you a clear first step.

A script for the first phone call

You can use three sentences on the phone. Tell them where the water is and where it came from. Ask what you should do right now while they arrange a crew. Ask what the first visit includes and how the scope will be written down.

A good dispatcher gives you the first step, such as closing the valve or leaving a room, before talking about the visit. If all you get is a sales pitch, call another company.

What to have ready when the crew arrives

  • A clear path to the wet rooms
  • The location of the main water valve and the electrical panel
  • Your insurance company name and policy number
  • A list of any health concerns in the home
  • Your photos of the damage so far

Having these ready saves time on site and lets the crew spend its first minutes on the water instead of the paperwork.

Checking references and reviews carefully

Ask for the names of past customers who agree to talk, or look for written feedback on neutral sites. Treat a perfect record with caution and read how a company responds to complaints. A calm, specific reply tells you more than a star count.

Trusting your read of the conversation

After the call, ask yourself whether you understood the answers. Plain language, specific numbers and a willingness to put things in writing are all good signs. Vague answers or pressure to decide on the spot are reasons to keep looking, even when you are in a hurry.

A Windsor note: check before you sign

After storms, out-of-area crews sometimes go door to door. Look up any license on the Contractors State License Board website and read the contract before you sign. California requires a contractor's license for work of $1,000 or more in labor and materials, according to the board.

Sonoma County emergency managers publish recovery information at socoemergency.org, including permit notes after storms. Check it if your damage is part of a wider event.

Questions readers ask

Do restoration companies need a license?

In California, contractors need a license for jobs of $1,000 or more in labor and materials. Check the Contractors State License Board website for the license you are given.

Should I get several quotes?

If time allows, yes. In an emergency, start drying with one company and compare the rebuild quotes later.

Is an hourly rate a red flag?

Not by itself. Ask for an estimate of hours and a cap or a check-in point.

Can the company guarantee insurance payment?

No. Only your insurer decides coverage.

What should Windsor homeowners do about door-to-door offers?

Say no thanks, look up the company yourself and verify the license before you sign anything.
