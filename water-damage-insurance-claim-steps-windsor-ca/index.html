Water Damage Insurance Claims: Documentation and Steps in Windsor, CA

A practical order of operations for the first hour, the first week and the weeks that follow.

By the Windsor Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

A water damage claim goes better when the record is built from the first hour. This guide walks through the steps in the order they usually happen, from stopping the water to the final payment. It is general information and does not replace your policy or your insurer's instructions.

You do not need special skills for most of it. You need a phone, a notebook and the habit of writing down who you spoke to and when.

The first hour

  1. Stop the source. Close a fixture valve or the main valve.
  2. Make the home safe. Keep clear of wet electrical equipment.
  3. Photograph and film every wet room before moving anything.
  4. Call a restoration company so drying can start.
  5. Report the loss to your insurer and ask for a claim number.

Do not wait to finish the paperwork before drying begins. Insurers generally expect you to protect the property from more damage, and a day of delay can change what can be saved.

What to photograph and record

Take wide shots of each room, then close shots of the damage, the source and the moisture. A tape measure in the frame shows the height of a waterline. Film a slow walk through the house, saying the date and room aloud.

Photograph damaged belongings individually, including labels and serial numbers. Keep a list with the item, its age and what you paid for it. Receipts, bank statements and online order histories help prove value.

Keep damaged items when you can

An adjuster may want to see a failed part, such as a split hose or a corroded fitting. Put it in a bag and label it. Do not throw out large damaged items such as carpet or a soaked mattress until your insurer says you may, or until you have documented them well.

If something must go for safety reasons, such as items soaked by sewage, photograph it first and tell your insurer why it was removed.

Working with the adjuster

The adjuster looks at the cause, the scope and the cost. Walk them through the damage and give them your photos and the restoration company's moisture readings. Keep a copy of everything you hand over.

You can ask questions about what is and is not covered, and you can ask for decisions in writing. If you disagree with a decision, ask for the reasons and the policy language behind it.

Estimates, scopes and supplements

Restoration companies and insurers can differ on scope. A mitigation scope covers water removal and drying, and a rebuild scope covers repairs after drying. If drying shows more damage than the first look did, the scope can grow through a supplement that your insurer reviews.

Ask for the scope in writing and keep it with your claim file. The structural drying page explains why readings drive the length of a job.

California timelines in plain terms

California claims regulations set deadlines for insurers. Under Title 10 of the California Code of Regulations, an insurer generally must acknowledge notice of a claim within 15 calendar days, and must accept or deny a claim within 40 calendar days after receiving proof of claim. If it accepts, payment is generally due within 30 calendar days.

These are general rules, and exceptions exist. If your insurer is slow or unclear, put your questions in writing and ask for a reply. The California Department of Insurance accepts consumer complaints about claim handling.

Keep a claim log

Start a simple log on the first day. Write the date, the person you spoke to, what they said and what comes next. Save emails and note the dates of letters.

The log helps you remember, helps you follow up and helps if there is ever a dispute. It also gives you a record of emergency costs, so you can request reimbursement.

After the check arrives

When payment comes, compare it to the scope and your receipts. If the amount seems low, ask for the line-item breakdown. Keep every statement, because a supplemental request may follow once drying shows what the first look missed. Pay contractors against written invoices, and keep proof of each payment with the rest of the claim file.

A Windsor note: when everyone files at once

After a widespread storm, many households file in the same weeks, and insurers can be slower to respond. A clear photo record and a written log help your file stand out, which matters most when adjusters are busy.

If your loss involves a rebuild, call the Town of Windsor building counter before work starts to learn whether permits are needed, and keep any permit paperwork with your claim file.

Questions readers ask

Should I clean up before the adjuster visits?

Stop the water and start drying, but photograph first. Keep damaged items and failed parts if you can, and ask your insurer before you discard large items.

Who pays the restoration company?

That depends on your policy and agreement. Many homeowners pay and are reimbursed. Ask your insurer and read the work order before you sign.

Can I choose my own restoration company?

Generally yes. Your insurer may suggest vendors, but you usually choose who does the work. Confirm with your insurer.

What if the insurer denies the claim?

Ask for the denial in writing with the policy language. You can ask for a review and file a complaint with the California Department of Insurance.

What should Windsor homeowners keep from a storm loss?

Keep photos of the exterior and interior, any roofer's report, receipts for emergency steps and permit records from the Town of Windsor.
