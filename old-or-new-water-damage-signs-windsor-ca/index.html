How to Tell if Water Damage Is Old or New: A Windsor, CA Homeowner Guide

What stains, textures and moisture readings reveal about when and how long water has been present.

By the Windsor Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

A stain on a ceiling raises one question: is this still happening? Old damage that has dried needs a different response from a leak that is active today. This guide explains the visible clues and the limits of judging by eye.

The most reliable answer comes from measuring, not looking. A moisture meter and a thermal camera can tell you what a stain cannot.

Clues that suggest older, dry damage

  • Brown or tan rings with a sharp, dry edge
  • Paint that has already peeled and hardened
  • Stains that look the same over several weeks
  • Warped boards that feel stable and dry
  • No odor and no cool, damp feeling to the surface

A ring that does not change after rain or after using the shower is often old. Rings form when a stain dries and leaves minerals behind. Multiple rings can mean the area has gotten wet more than once.

Clues that suggest new or active moisture

  • A stain that is darker in the middle and has a fuzzy, wet edge
  • Paint that is bubbling or feels soft
  • A surface that is cool or damp to the touch
  • Drips, beads of water or a sound of running water
  • A stain that grows when it rains, when a fixture runs or when the heat comes on

Mark the edge of a stain with a pencil and write the date. If it grows in a day or two, you probably have an active source. Take a photo each day so you can compare.

Why looking is not enough

Stains can mislead. An old stain can look fresh if humidity is high, and a new leak can leave almost no visible mark for a while. Paint covers evidence, and some leaks wet insulation and framing without staining the surface at all.

That is why professionals measure. A pin or pinless moisture meter reads moisture in drywall, wood and subfloors. A thermal imaging camera shows cooler patches where moisture evaporates. Together they show whether the area is wet right now.

Old damage can still matter

A dry stain does not always mean a harmless one. Materials that stayed wet for a long time can lose strength, and past leaks can leave odors. Rusted fasteners, soft framing or darkened wood point to long exposure.

If you see these signs, read the wall and drywall page and the ceiling page for what repair looks like.

What to do if you cannot tell

  1. Mark the edge of the stain and date it.
  2. Photograph it from the same spot every day for a week.
  3. Run fixtures one at a time to see if the stain responds.
  4. Note what happens after rain.
  5. Call for a reading if it grows or feels damp.

If you are buying or selling a home, a moisture inspection is cheaper than discovering hidden damage after closing. Ask a qualified inspector or a restoration company for readings.

Insurance and age of damage

Insurers pay attention to how long damage has been there, because sudden damage is often covered and long-term seepage is often excluded. Photos with dates, repair records and moisture readings help show the timeline. Always check your policy.

Roof, plumbing or humidity

Once you know the damage is active, the next step is finding the source. A stain below a bathroom points to plumbing. A stain on a top-floor ceiling that grows after rain points to the roof. A damp wall near a window may mean a failed seal or flashing.

The storm water damage page covers rain entering through the building envelope.

A photo routine that helps

Use the same spot, the same distance and the same light each time you photograph a stain. Add a coin or a ruler for scale. A week of dated photos tells you more than a month of guessing, and it also gives your insurer a timeline.

Buying or selling with a past leak

Past water damage is common in older homes and is not always a deal breaker. Ask for repair invoices, find out whether the source was fixed and look at moisture readings in the affected rooms. Sellers who can show a clean record of repair usually have an easier time, and buyers who ask early avoid surprises after closing.

A Windsor note: reading stains after the wet season

Windsor's rain arrives in winter, and roof leaks often show up a few days after a storm, not during it. A stain that appears after a long wet spell deserves a look at the attic and the flashing.

If the stain sits below an attic, ask a roofer to check flashing, vents and shingles, and confirm the roofer's credentials with the state licensing board before hiring.

Questions readers ask

Does a brown ring always mean an old leak?

No. It means water was present and dried. A dry ring can be old, but a wet ring with a soft edge is active.

Can a stain be painted over?

Only after the area is dry and the source is repaired. Painting a wet stain tends to bubble and bleed through.

Can I use a cheap moisture meter at home?

It can give a rough idea, but readings depend on the material and the meter. Professional meters and thermal cameras give more reliable results.

What if I find old damage when buying a home?

Ask the seller for repair records and have a qualified inspector check moisture. Get any repair scope in writing.

How do Windsor homeowners separate roof leaks from plumbing leaks?

Check timing. A stain that grows after rain suggests the roof, while one that grows when a fixture runs suggests plumbing.
