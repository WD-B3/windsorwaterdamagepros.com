Emergency Commercial Water Damage in Windsor, CA

Water in a shop, office, restaurant or multi-unit building in Windsor. We extract, dry and document so you can reopen sooner and file a clean claim.

When water gets into a business, every hour of downtime counts. A burst supply line, a sprinkler discharge, a roof leak or a backed-up drain can close a store, office or restaurant for days. Business owners in Windsor and the surrounding county can reach a dispatcher at (707) 221-5171 to describe the building and the loss.

This page is for businesses, multi-unit buildings and managed properties. A commercial loss involves tenants, landlords, insurers and sometimes health or safety rules, so the plan covers more than drying.

When water hits a business

  1. Protect people first. Clear the area and keep staff and customers out of wet or hazardous spaces.
  2. Shut off the water source or the building main, and shut off power to wet areas only if it is safe.
  3. Call dispatch and describe the building, the water source and whether other tenants are affected.
  4. Photograph the damage, record inventory and equipment that got wet and note the time the loss started.
  5. Notify the property manager or landlord and your insurer.

Electrical and sprinkler systems

Water near panels, point-of-sale systems and refrigeration equipment is a shock risk. Do not touch wet electrical equipment. If a fire sprinkler has discharged, contact the fire protection company and the fire department as needed, and call 911 for any electrical or fire concern.

Windsor business areas and what they mean for water loss

Windsor's commercial life centers on a few areas: the downtown around the Town Green, the Lakewood shopping area, the Shiloh shopping area and the Old Redwood Highway corridor. Each has a mix of tenant spaces that share walls, ceilings and plumbing, so a leak in one unit often reaches its neighbor.

If your space shares a wall or a roof with another tenant, tell dispatch so crews can check adjoining units and so each tenant's insurer has a clear record. Check your lease for who is responsible for building systems and who files the claim.

How commercial restoration is organized

Commercial jobs are staged to keep as much of the business running as possible. Crews can work by section, extracting and drying the wettest areas first, setting containment around occupied space and running equipment after hours where noise is a concern.

Moisture mapping covers floors, walls, ceilings and any inventory or equipment on the floor. Readings and photos are recorded daily, which supports business interruption and property claims. The drying approach follows the same standard described on the structural drying page.

Multi-unit and managed buildings

In apartments and managed buildings, water travels between units through floors, walls and plumbing chases. Property managers should report the loss to their insurer promptly, notify affected tenants and keep a log of units visited. A restoration company can provide an itemized scope for the insurer and a record of readings for each unit.

Questions about commercial water damage

Does your line cover shops and offices after closing time?

Yes. Call (707) 221-5171 after hours and tell dispatch the building type, the floors affected and whether tenants share the space. We scope the loss in writing.

Can you work while my business stays open?

Often, yes. Work can be staged by section, with equipment placed to leave unaffected areas open, though some losses require closing the affected space.

Do you help with insurance documentation?

We provide photos, readings and an itemized scope. Your insurer decides coverage and what the policy pays.

Who is responsible, the landlord or the tenant?

Your lease decides. Read it and ask your landlord, property manager and insurer who files and pays for what.

Do you handle sprinkler discharge?

We handle the water damage. A fire protection company repairs and resets the sprinkler system.
