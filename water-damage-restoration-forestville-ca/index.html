Forestville is a census-designated place in Sonoma County, with 3,264 residents at the 2020 census and ZIP code 95436. Near the town, the Laguna de Santa Rosa and Mark West Creek meet the Russian River.

The wet season in Forestville

Rain in this part of Sonoma County comes in winter, and a stretch of heavy storms tests every roof, gutter and crawl space. In January 2023, county emergency managers reported between 5 and 20 inches of rain across Sonoma County during the first nineteen days of the month.

A house near a creek or river sees water in more ways than a hillside house does. Rain can come through the roof, groundwater can rise under the floor and outside water can enter at doors. Start with the storm water damage page when water came through the building, and the flood damage page when it came from the ground.

What the river gauge tells you

NOAA's California Nevada River Forecast Center publishes stages for the Russian River at Guerneville, downstream of Forestville. Flood stage is 32 feet there, and the record crest was 49.5 feet on February 18, 1986. A 2025 crest of 38.79 feet on February 5 sits in the moderate flood range, and the January 2023 crest was 31.83 feet.

These numbers describe the river at that gauge, not your address. Your own parcel is what counts. Look the address up on the FEMA map, ask Permit Sonoma about local rules and review your flood coverage well before January.

Older buildings and what water finds in them

Forestville was settled in the late 1860s, and the downtown blocks were developed in the 1870s and 1880s. A fire destroyed many older structures, and most buildings that remain date to the early twentieth century. If your building is old, check the plumbing material, the sill plate and the crawl space.

Water can sit under an old floor for weeks without showing. The crawl space page and the structural drying page explain how we find and dry it. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show the extent without tearing out everything.

When water is spreading in Forestville

Call first, then act. Dispatch will talk you through the shutoff and ask a few questions about the water. If it came from outside, tell us. If a drain is backing up, keep everyone away from it.

Contaminated water

Water from a river, creek, toilet or drain can carry bacteria and debris. Do not wade through it more than needed, and keep children and pets away. Wash your hands after contact.

Before we arrive: a checklist for river-country homes

Shut the water off

Locate the main valve before the storm season, so that you are not hunting for it in the dark.

Watch the electrics

Do not touch a panel or outlet if water is near. Call the utility and 911 for any hazard.

Go high

Stack valuables on a high shelf or upstairs. Documents and photos go first.

Stay out of floodwater

River water carries contaminants. Keep children, pets and bare skin out.

Document the line

Photograph the waterline on walls with a tape measure in the frame.

Permits, insurance and repairs

Because Forestville has no city government, permits come from Permit Sonoma, which sits at 2550 Ventura Avenue in Santa Rosa. Flood is the exclusion that surprises river-country owners most, so read your policy before the wet season and call your insurer before deciding what to claim.

Is emergency water removal available in Forestville?

Do you cover every ZIP code in Forestville?

We serve ZIP 95436 for Forestville, including homes along River Road and near the Russian River. Call if you are unsure whether your address is in range.

Is river flooding covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually not. Flood is typically excluded and needs a separate flood policy. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

How do I check my flood zone?

Use the FEMA map lookup for your address, and ask Permit Sonoma whether the parcel sits in a mapped hazard area.
