Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Windsor, CA

Cupping, buckling and dark stains in a Windsor wood or laminate floor. We dry floors slowly and say honestly when boards need to be replaced.

Call (707) 221-5171

Wood and water do not mix, but they do not always have to end in replacement. Hardwood floors absorb water at the edges and from below, then swell, cup or buckle as they dry unevenly. Wet boards are a reason to ring (707) 221-5171 quickly, since slow, controlled drying protects the most wood.

This page covers wood and laminate flooring. Carpet and pad are on a separate page, and the leak that caused the damage is a job for a plumber. How fast the floor is dried is the biggest factor in how much of it is saved.

Cupping, buckling and dark stains

Cupping is when board edges rise higher than the center, usually because moisture came from below or one side. Crowning is the opposite, and buckling is when boards lift off the subfloor. Dark stains, black rings and gaps between boards can mean water has been present for some time.

A cupped floor is not always ruined. If it is dried slowly under controlled conditions, many boards flatten, and sanding and refinishing can finish the job. If the boards are buckled or the finish has delaminated, replacement of the affected section is more likely.

What to do when a wood floor gets wet

  1. Stop the water source and remove standing water with towels or a wet vacuum.
  2. Lift rugs, mats and furniture off the floor and remove anything that holds water.
  3. Do not use a heater or a hair dryer to dry the boards fast. Fast drying can crack boards.
  4. Photograph the floor and any staining.
  5. Call dispatch for moisture readings and controlled drying.

How wood floors are dried

The crew takes moisture readings in the boards, subfloor and baseboards, then sets air movers and dehumidifiers to dry the floor at a controlled pace. In many cases, small holes or removed boards let air reach the cavity under the floor. Mats and drying systems can pull moisture out through the boards when the floor is bonded.

Drying wood takes days and sometimes longer, and boards often continue to move as they dry. This is why the crew waits for readings to level out before anyone sands or refinishes. See structural drying for the full approach.

Floors laid over a slab or in a crawl space home behave differently, because moisture can come from below. If the subfloor is wet, the crawl space page and the slab leak page cover those sources.

When to repair and when to replace

A floor is a candidate for repair when boards have lightly cupped, the finish is intact and the subfloor is dry. It is a candidate for replacement when boards are buckled, the finish has peeled, the core has crumbled or the floor was wet for many days. A moisture reading and a written scope help you and your insurer see which applies.

Match matters too. Replacement boards may not match older flooring, so ask whether a whole room needs to be refinished to blend.

Questions about hardwood floor water damage

Is a wet wood floor worth a middle-of-the-night call?

If water is still on the boards, yes. Mop up what you can, lift rugs and call (707) 221-5171 so controlled drying can start before boards cup.

Can a cupped floor flatten on its own?

Sometimes, once it dries slowly and evenly. Do not sand until readings are stable.

Will insurance cover my floor?

Sudden water damage is often covered and gradual leaks often are not. Check your policy, photograph the floor and ask your insurer.

Is laminate saved the same way?

Usually not. Fiberboard cores swell and do not return, so laminate is often replaced.

How long does drying take?

Several days is common for wood floors, and sometimes longer. Readings, not a date, tell you when it is done.
