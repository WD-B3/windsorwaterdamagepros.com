Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Windsor, CA

A pipe froze, split and is now thawing into your Windsor walls, attic or garage. Shut off the water, then call dispatch for extraction and drying.

Call (707) 221-5171

Frozen pipe water damage often shows up hours after the pipe froze. Ice blocks the line and can split the pipe wall, but the water only escapes once the ice melts and pressure returns. That is why a quiet cold night can turn into a flooded wall at breakfast. If a thaw has started a leak, call (707) 221-5171 and dispatch will walk you through the shutoff and the first cleanup steps.

We do not have a Windsor figure for how many freezing nights a typical winter brings, and this page does not claim that frozen pipes are common here. What we can say is what happens when it does occur. When a pipe in an attic, garage or exterior wall freezes, the damage can be large because those spaces are hidden and often unheated.

If you suspect a pipe froze or split

  1. Shut off the main water valve before anything thaws, especially if a faucet has stopped running or only drips.
  2. Open a faucet on the affected line so melting water has a place to go instead of the split.
  3. Do not use an open flame or a propane torch to thaw pipe. A hair dryer on low heat is safer, but only when you can see no active leak.
  4. Look in the attic, garage, crawl space and exterior walls for wet insulation, dripping or ceiling stains.
  5. Photograph damage and call dispatch for extraction and drying.

Ceilings and wiring

If water is dripping from light fixtures or the ceiling is bulging, keep out of that room. Wet insulation can load a ceiling with far more weight than it was built to carry. Call 911 for any electrical hazard before calling the restoration line.

Why the damage appears after the thaw

Water expands about nine percent when it freezes, and that pressure can split copper, crack plastic fittings or pop a joint. The pipe may look intact until the ice melts, and a small crack may seep for a day before it grows. The first sign is often a damp patch on a ceiling or a sound of running water in a quiet house.

Because the leak starts in a hidden space, moisture readings matter more here than in a visible leak. Crews check attic insulation, ceiling drywall, wall cavities and the floor below. The thermal imaging camera is useful because a cold, wet cavity shows clearly against a dry one.

Cleanup and drying after a frozen pipe split

The crew removes standing water, pulls out saturated insulation and opens ceiling or wall sections where moisture is trapped. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the framing and surfaces that can be saved. Insulation that has been soaked is usually replaced because it holds water and loses its thermal value.

The pipe repair belongs to a licensed plumber, and the restoration plan should be built around when walls can safely be closed. For the drying process in detail, the structural drying page explains equipment and moisture readings.

If a pipe split for another reason, the cleanup steps are nearly the same. The difference is where the water traveled and how long it ran before anyone noticed.

Preventing a repeat

Insulate pipes in unheated spaces, keep garage doors closed on cold nights and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so room heat reaches the pipes. If you are leaving home for several days, ask a plumber about shutting off and draining the system. For a longer checklist, the Windsor homeowner guide on leaving home for a few days is on the blog.

Questions about frozen pipe damage

Should I call before or after a frozen pipe thaws?

Call as soon as you suspect a split. Thawing is when water appears, and dispatch can talk you through the valve and the first cleanup at (707) 221-5171, any hour.

Are frozen pipes common in Windsor?

We do not have a local freeze figure, so we do not claim they are common or rare. When a hard freeze does reach a home, pipes in attics, garages and exterior walls are the usual victims.

Should I thaw the pipe myself?

Only if you can see no active leak and the main is off. Never use a torch or open flame. If the pipe has already split, thawing it will release water, so call first.

Is my insurance likely to cover it?

Sudden pipe bursts are often covered, but ask your insurer about freeze conditions and any steps the policy expects, such as keeping the home heated.

How do I know water is hiding in my walls?

Look for ceiling stains, warm or cold wall patches, peeling paint and a musty odor. Moisture meters and thermal imaging confirm what the eye cannot.
