Emergency Appliance Leak Water Damage in Windsor, CA

A washing machine, dishwasher or refrigerator line has leaked in your Windsor home. We remove the water and dry the floor and walls around the appliance.

Call (707) 221-5171

Appliance leaks are quiet and expensive. A washing machine supply hose can fail while you are at work, a dishwasher drain can drip behind a toe kick for weeks and a refrigerator water line can leak under the floor without leaving a visible puddle. Describe the machine and the puddle to dispatch at (707) 221-5171 and we will tell you what to shut off and what to photograph.

This page is about the appliance and its supply or drain connections. Cabinets, countertops and the sink area are covered on the kitchen page, and the cause of the failure is a repair job for an appliance technician or plumber.

First steps when an appliance is leaking

  1. Close the water supply valve to the appliance, usually behind or beside it. For a washer, that means both hot and cold valves.
  2. Unplug the appliance only if the cord and outlet are dry and you can reach them without stepping in water. Otherwise turn off the breaker for that circuit.
  3. Pull the appliance out only if it is safe, and lift rugs and boxes off wet flooring.
  4. Photograph the hose, the label with the model number and the wet area.
  5. Call dispatch for water removal and drying, and call an appliance technician for the repair.

Water near outlets

Many laundry and kitchen outlets sit near the floor. If water has reached an outlet or the appliance is still plugged in and sitting in water, stay back, turn off power at the breaker only if you can reach it dry and call 911 for any shock or spark.

Where appliance leaks come from

Washing machine hoses are the usual suspect because rubber ages and fittings loosen. Dishwashers leak at door gaskets, drain hoses and inlet valves. Refrigerators and ice makers use small supply lines that can crack or come loose behind the unit, and water heaters, which are covered on a separate page, are a different source again.

Many Windsor homes have laundry rooms or kitchens built into interior walls, so an appliance leak can run into the wall cavity and the flooring on the other side. Water that disappears under baseboard or toe kick plate has often not gone away, so moisture readings are the way to be sure.

How the crew handles an appliance leak

The crew pulls out standing water, checks moisture in the flooring, baseboard, cabinet bases and wall behind the appliance and opens small access points when readings show water inside a wall. Vinyl and laminate can trap water beneath the surface, so edges and seams are checked closely.

Drying follows with air movers and dehumidifiers. Cabinet toe kicks are often removed so air can reach the base, and wet insulation behind a washer wall is removed. Readings are repeated until materials reach a dry standard, which prevents the musty odor and swelling that show up weeks later.

If the appliance is on an upper floor, water can travel through the subfloor into a ceiling below. See the ceiling water damage page for that path.

Gray water and cleanup choices

Water from a washer drain or dishwasher can carry detergent and food residue, so crews treat it with more care than clean supply water. Under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard it is usually classed as gray water, and porous materials that soaked in it for long may be replaced instead of dried. Water from a supply line is cleaner, and flooring caught within a day often dries in place.

Questions about appliance leaks

Is an overnight washer or dishwasher leak an emergency call?

It can be. Water spreads under cabinets and into walls fast, so shut the appliance valve and call dispatch. Photos of the floor and the hose help your insurer later.

Is a washing machine leak covered by insurance?

A sudden hose failure is often covered, while a slow leak that went unfixed may not be. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and keep photos of the hose and the damage.

How do I know if a leak is hiding under my floor?

Soft or warped flooring, a musty smell near the appliance and a floor that feels cool or damp are signs. Moisture meters confirm what the eye cannot.

Can I just replace the hose and mop up?

Replace the hose, yes. But mopping rarely dries the wall base, subfloor and insulation, and hidden moisture is what causes swelling and odor later.

Who fixes the appliance?

An appliance technician or plumber repairs the appliance. We handle water removal, drying and the plan for repairs to what the water damaged.
