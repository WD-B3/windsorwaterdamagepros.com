How to Save Photos and Important Documents After Water Damage (Windsor, CA)

Practical first steps for wet paper, photographs and records, and how to replace what cannot be saved.

By the Windsor Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

Wet photographs and papers feel urgent because they are. Paper weakens fast, and photos stick together as they dry. The first day matters, and so does handling them gently.

This guide covers common steps. For irreplaceable items, a conservator can give better advice than a general guide.

First, make sure it is safe to handle

If the water was clean, you can handle items with clean hands. If it was sewage or flood water, wear gloves and consider whether the item is worth the risk. Contaminated paper is harder to clean and may not be worth saving unless it is irreplaceable.

Wet photographs

  1. Do not touch the surface of the image.
  2. Gently rinse with clean, cool water if mud is present.
  3. Separate photos that are stuck together by soaking them in clean water, then peeling gently.
  4. Lay them face up on clean paper towels or hang them by a corner.
  5. Keep them out of direct sunlight and away from heat.

Photos that are stuck and cannot be separated can be frozen in a bag until you can get help. Freezing buys time.

Wet documents and books

Blot with clean paper and stand books upright with pages fanned. Interleave pages with paper towels if you have time. A box fan on low helps air movement.

If you cannot deal with them right away, freeze them. A freezer stops further damage and lets you dry things later. Use a frost-free freezer carefully, because the cycle can dry items unevenly.

What not to do

  • Do not peel stuck pages apart when dry
  • Do not use a hair dryer, oven or microwave
  • Do not use bleach on paper
  • Do not leave wet items piled together
  • Do not use a clothes iron

Important documents and replacement

If you cannot save a document, you can usually request a replacement. Birth, marriage and property records are held by government offices. Many banks and insurers can reissue statements and policy documents.

Make a list of what was damaged, and scan or photograph what you saved. Store copies in the cloud and in a second location, such as a safe deposit box.

Professional help

Some restoration companies offer document drying, often using freeze-drying methods. Ask whether yours does, or ask for a referral. Conservators handle valuable or historic items.

Our crews focus on the structure, but we can tell you what to do with your belongings in the first hours. The water extraction page explains the structure side.

Back up for next time

Scan important documents and photos. Keep the originals in a waterproof container or a high shelf. A few minutes of preparation can save a lot of grief after a leak.

Setting up a drying station

Choose a room that did not get wet. Cover a table with clean paper towels or unprinted newsprint. Lay photos face up in a single layer, and place documents flat with a sheet of blotting paper between pages when you can.

Change the absorbent paper as it gets wet. A fan on low, pointed across the room rather than at the items, keeps the air moving. Avoid direct sun, which can fade images.

What a freeze plan looks like

  1. Wrap small groups of wet photos in wax paper.
  2. Place them in a freezer bag and press out the air.
  3. Label the bag with the date and contents.
  4. Freeze flat if you can.
  5. Thaw and dry a few at a time when you are ready.

Freezing slows decay and keeps pages from sticking while you deal with the rest of the house. It is a pause button, not a fix.

Digital backups worth making now

A phone scan is good enough for most family photos. Make a folder for identity documents, insurance policies, tax papers and property records, and keep a copy in cloud storage. Update it once a year.

If you are replacing a lost document, a scan of the damaged original can help the agency confirm what you need.

Sentimental items and what to expect

Some photos will not come back looking perfect. Emulsion can lift, colors can shift and edges can curl. A careful scan of what remains, followed by gentle retouching, can give you a good copy even when the original is marked.

Take time with this part. It is easy to rush when emotions run high, and slow, patient handling saves more than speed does.

Working with a conservator or restorer

Before you hand over valuable pieces, ask what methods will be used, how long the work takes and what the realistic outcome is. Get a written receipt that lists each item and its condition. Ask whether the work is reversible, since the best treatments protect the original.

A Windsor note: where to get copies

In Sonoma County, copies of recorded property documents can be requested from the Sonoma County Clerk-Recorder-Assessor. Vital records such as birth and marriage certificates are also handled through county offices, so check their website for current steps.

If your loss follows a wider storm, the county emergency site at socoemergency.org may list recovery resources. Keep your claim number handy when you call.

Questions readers ask

How long do I have to save wet photos?

Act as soon as possible. Within a day or two is best, and freezing stops further damage.

Can I air dry photos on paper towels?

Yes, face up on clean paper towels, away from sun and heat.

Should I use a hair dryer?

No. Heat can warp and stick images.

Who restores valuable documents?

A conservator or a document restoration company. Ask your insurer whether it covers the cost.

Where do Windsor residents get replacement records?

Start with the Sonoma County Clerk-Recorder-Assessor for property records and the county for vital records.
