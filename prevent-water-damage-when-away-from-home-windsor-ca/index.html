How to Prevent Water Damage When You Leave Home: A Windsor, CA Checklist

A short checklist to run before a trip so that a small leak does not become a week of running water.

By the Windsor Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

The worst leaks happen when nobody is home. A split hose that runs for an hour is a mess, and one that runs for a week is a rebuild. This checklist takes about ten minutes and cuts the risk.

You do not need to do everything. Even closing the main valve and asking a neighbor to look in changes the odds.

Close the main valve

The simplest step is shutting off the water where it enters the house. With the main closed, a failed hose or fitting cannot flood the house. Learn where the valve is before you need it, and test that it turns.

Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house after closing the main to drain the lines. When you come back, open the valve slowly and check for drips.

Check the high-risk spots

SpotWhat to checkWhy it fails
Washing machine hosesCracks, bulges and loose couplingsRubber hoses age and burst under pressure
Dishwasher supply lineDrips under the sinkFittings loosen over time
Refrigerator ice maker lineKinks and damp floor behindSmall tubing can split
Water heaterRust, drips and a working pan drainTanks corrode and valves stick
Under-sink supply linesCorrosion and moistureCompression fittings age
Toilet supply and flapperDamp floor and running waterSlow leaks grow

Replace old rubber hoses with braided ones if yours are cracked. The water heater page explains what to look for on a tank.

Set the house for the season

In the wet season, clear gutters and downspouts, check that water drains away from the foundation and look for loose shingles. In cold snaps, keep the house heated enough to protect pipes and open cabinet doors on exterior walls.

The frozen pipe page covers what cold weather can do to exposed lines. Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses before you go.

Ask a person to check in

A neighbor, friend or relative who can walk through every few days is the best leak detector. Give them a key, tell them where the main valve is and give them the phone numbers for a plumber and a restoration company.

If you cannot arrange a visitor, consider a water leak sensor. Many cost little and send phone alerts. Place them under sinks, near the water heater and behind the washing machine.

Consider an automatic shutoff

Some homes have valves that close automatically when a sensor detects water or unusual flow. A plumber can install one. Ask your insurer whether it affects your premium.

If you come home to water

  1. Stay out if water is near electrical equipment.
  2. Close the main valve if you can reach it safely.
  3. Photograph everything before you move it.
  4. Call a restoration company and your insurer.
  5. Keep receipts for emergency costs.

Water that ran for days has soaked deeper than water that ran for hours. Tell dispatch how long you were gone.

Insurance and vacancies

Some policies limit coverage if a home is vacant for a stated number of days. A short trip rarely triggers it, but a long one might. Ask your agent how your policy treats absences.

A printable pre-trip list

  • Close the main water valve
  • Check washer, dishwasher and refrigerator lines
  • Set the water heater to vacation mode if it has one
  • Clear gutters and downspouts in the wet season
  • Leave the heat on low in cold weather
  • Give a trusted person a key and the shutoff location
  • Save the dispatch number in your phone: (707) 221-5171

Tape a copy of the list inside a cabinet door. When the trip is a hurry, the list does the remembering for you.

Common mistakes before a trip

People often shut off the main valve but forget the irrigation timer, which can keep running from a separate line. Others leave a washing machine mid-cycle or a dishwasher on a delay timer. Walk through each room once and ask what could run while you are gone.

Another mistake is relying on a neighbor without telling them where the valve is. A five-minute walk-through before you leave is worth it.

A Windsor note: wet-season trips and county alerts

Winter is when Windsor roofs, gutters and crawl spaces are tested, and the January 2023 storms are a reminder that heavy rain can arrive in a run. If you travel in the wet months, expect weather to find any weak spot while you are gone.

Sonoma County runs an emergency information site at socoemergency.org where residents can learn about alerts. Register before you travel so warnings reach you even when you are away.

Questions readers ask

Should I shut off the water heater too?

For longer trips, many people turn the water heater to vacation or off. Follow the manufacturer's instructions, and close the cold supply to the tank only if told to.

Are leak sensors worth it?

They are inexpensive and provide early warning. Place them near the water heater, under sinks and behind the washer.

Who should have a key?

Someone you trust who can check the house and knows where the main valve is.

How often should a neighbor check?

Every two or three days is a common arrangement. For long absences, more often is better.

What do Windsor residents do about storm alerts when away?

Sign up for county emergency alerts through the Sonoma County emergency site and make sure someone local can check on the house after heavy rain.
