How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your House: A Windsor, CA Guide

Simple checks you can do with a meter, a flashlight and a notebook, and when to bring in help.

By the Windsor Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

Many serious leaks start where nobody can see them. Water runs inside a wall, under a slab or above a ceiling and shows up only after the damage is done. This guide gives you checks to try before calling anyone.

None of these replace a professional leak detection service, but they can tell you whether you have a leak and roughly where to look.

Start with the water meter

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation system in the house and make sure no one is flushing.
  2. Find the water meter, usually in a ground-level box near the street or the front of the lot.
  3. Look at the leak indicator, often a small triangle or dial that spins when water moves.
  4. Write down the reading, wait an hour without using water and read it again.
  5. If the reading changed, water is moving somewhere.

If the meter moves with everything off, the leak is somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. If it does not move, the leak may be on a fixture or may be intermittent.

Check the bills

Compare recent water bills with the same months last year. A steady rise with no change in habits is a warning. A sudden jump suggests something failed. Irrigation and guests explain many spikes, so rule those out first.

Your water provider can often help you read the meter or check usage patterns. Ask what tools they offer.

Check the toilets and fixtures

Toilets are a frequent source of silent water loss. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color shows in the bowl after fifteen minutes without flushing, the flapper leaks. Check under sinks for drips or damp cabinet floors.

Look at appliance hoses on the washer, dishwasher and refrigerator. A bulging hose or a damp spot underneath is a sign. The appliance leak page explains the common failures.

Signs inside the house

  • Stains, bubbling paint or peeling wallpaper
  • Warm spots on the floor that may indicate a hot water line leak under a slab
  • The sound of running water when everything is off
  • A musty smell in a closet, cabinet or room
  • Floors that feel soft, cupped or springy
  • Baseboards that swell or separate from the wall

A warm floor spot or a constant hissing can point to a slab leak. The slab leak page explains what that involves.

Signs outside the house

Look for wet patches in the yard when it has not rained, unusually green strips of lawn, or soft ground along the line from the meter to the house. Check the foundation line for dampness. Overflowing sprinkler valve boxes can also hide leaks.

If you see water pooling near the meter, tell your water provider right away. The line from the meter to the street is usually theirs to fix.

Finding leaks in walls and ceilings

A thermal imaging camera shows temperature differences behind surfaces, and a moisture meter confirms wetness. Professionals combine the two with acoustic listening devices to locate pipes under pressure.

Cutting a small inspection hole is often better than guessing. A neat hole costs little to patch compared with widespread damage.

Who fixes what

A plumber repairs the pipe. A restoration company dries what the water wet. A leak detection specialist locates the problem if it is not obvious. Keep invoices from all three for your insurer.

A simple weekly habit

Read the meter on the same day each week, before and after a night when nobody uses water. Write both numbers in a notebook. A change in the overnight number shows up long before a stain does.

What to tell a plumber or detection specialist

Bring your notes. Tell them what the meter did during your test, which rooms felt warm or damp and when you first noticed anything. Photos of stains with dates help too. The more you can say about timing, the faster a specialist can narrow the search and the less cutting is needed.

A Windsor note: ground, age and the meter

Windsor sits on US 101 in the Russian River valley, and some homes in the original downtown blocks and along Old Redwood Highway are older, and their plumbing may be galvanized or mixed metal. Corrosion in older lines is a common reason a leak shows first inside a wall.

If you suspect a leak between the meter and your house, ask your water provider who is responsible for that section. Digging or replacing a line may need a permit, so check with Town of Windsor building staff first.

Questions readers ask

Can a hidden leak cause a high water bill?

Yes. A leak that runs all day adds up on the meter. Compare your bill with the same months last year and run the meter test.

How do I know if a leak is under the slab?

Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water and a moving meter with everything off are common signs. A detection specialist can confirm.

Is a hidden leak covered by insurance?

The resulting damage may be covered if the failure was sudden. The cost of finding the leak varies by policy. Ask your insurer.

What do Windsor homeowners do about a leak on the street side?

Contact your water provider. The line from the main to the meter is generally theirs.

Can I find a leak without cutting into the wall?

Often. Thermal imaging, moisture meters and listening devices can narrow it down, and a small hole may be needed to confirm.
